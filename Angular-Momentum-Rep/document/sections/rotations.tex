\section{Rotational Symmetry and Conservation Laws}

We establish the connection between the algebraic generators derived in the
previous section and the geometric symmetries of the physical system. This
quantizes the classical Noether's theorem: the invariance of the Hamiltonian
under spatial rotations implies the conservation of angular momentum.

\subsection{The Rotation Operator}

\subsubsection{Exponential Map}
Rotations in the Hilbert space are implemented by the unitary representation of
the group $\mathrm{SO}(3)$. For a rotation characterized by the vector $\boldsymbol{\theta}
= \theta \mathbf{n}$ (rotation by angle $\theta$ about axis $\mathbf{n}$), the
unitary operator is generated by $\hat{\mathbf{L}}$:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{R}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \exp\left(-\frac{i}{\hbar}
  \boldsymbol{\theta} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{L}}\right).
\end{equation}
Since $\hat{\mathbf{L}}$ is Hermitian, $\hat{R}$ is Unitary ($\hat{R}^\dagger
\hat{R} = \mathbb{I}$), preserving the normalization and probability currents of
the state.

\subsubsection{Transformation of Vector Operators}
A vector operator $\hat{\mathbf{V}}$ (such as position $\hat{\mathbf{r}}$ or
momentum $\hat{\mathbf{p}}$) is defined by its transformation properties under
the adjoint action of the rotation group. Using the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff
lemma, the components transform as:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{R}^\dagger(\boldsymbol{\theta}) \hat{V}_i \hat{R}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
  = \sum_{j} R_{ij}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) \hat{V}_j,
\end{equation}
where $R_{ij}$ are the components of the classical $3\times3$ rotation matrix.
Specifically for position eigenkets, this implies:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{R}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\ket{\mathbf{r}} = \ket{R\mathbf{r}}.
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Transformation of Scalar Fields}
The action of the rotation operator on a scalar wavefunction $\psi(\mathbf{r})$
is derived from the requirement that the physical state remains invariant under
a change of frame.
\begin{equation}
  \psi'(\mathbf{r}) = \braket{\mathbf{r}}{\psi'} = \bra{\mathbf{r}}
  \hat{R}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) \ket{\psi}.
\end{equation}
Inserting the identity $\int d^3r' \ket{\mathbf{r}'}\bra{\mathbf{r}'} =
\mathbb{I}$ and using $\braket{\mathbf{r}}{\mathbf{r}'} =
\delta(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}')$:
\begin{equation}
  \psi'(\mathbf{r}) = \psi(R^{-1}\mathbf{r}).
\end{equation}
For an infinitesimal rotation $d\boldsymbol{\theta}$, the inverse rotation is
$\mathbf{r} - d\boldsymbol{\theta} \times \mathbf{r}$. Expanding to first order:
\begin{align}
  \psi(\mathbf{r} - d\boldsymbol{\theta} \times \mathbf{r}) &\approx
  \psi(\mathbf{r}) - (d\boldsymbol{\theta} \times \mathbf{r}) \cdot \nabla
  \psi(\mathbf{r}) \nonumber \\
                                                            &= \psi(\mathbf{r}) - \frac{i}{\hbar} d\boldsymbol{\theta} \cdot (\mathbf{r}
                                                            \times -i\hbar\nabla) \psi(\mathbf{r}) \nonumber \\
                                                            &= \left( \mathbb{I} - \frac{i}{\hbar} d\boldsymbol{\theta} \cdot
                                                            \hat{\mathbf{L}} \right) \psi(\mathbf{r}).
\end{align}
This recovers the generator definition $\hat{\mathbf{L}} = \hat{\mathbf{r}}
\times \hat{\mathbf{p}}$ consistently from the field transformation perspective.

\subsection{Conservation Laws}

\subsubsection{Scalar Operators}
An operator $\hat{S}$ is termed a \textit{scalar operator} if it is invariant
under rotations, which implies it commutes with the generators:
\begin{equation}
  [\hat{L}_i, \hat{S}] = 0.
\end{equation}
The dot product of any two vector operators $\hat{\mathbf{A}} \cdot
\hat{\mathbf{B}}$ forms a scalar operator. We verify this generally using the
tensor commutator $[\hat{L}_i, \hat{A}_j] = i\hbar \epsilon_{ijk}\hat{A}_k$:
\begin{align}
  [\hat{L}_i, \hat{\mathbf{A}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{B}}] &= [\hat{L}_i, \hat{A}_j
  \hat{B}_j] \nonumber \\
                                                     &= [\hat{L}_i, \hat{A}_j]\hat{B}_j + \hat{A}_j[\hat{L}_i, \hat{B}_j]
                                                     \nonumber \\
                                                     &= i\hbar \epsilon_{ijk} \hat{A}_k \hat{B}_j + i\hbar \epsilon_{ijk}
                                                     \hat{A}_j \hat{B}_k.
\end{align}
Summing over $j,k$, the term $\hat{A}_k \hat{B}_j$ is symmetric (assuming
$[\hat{A},\hat{B}]=0$ or similar vector types) while $\epsilon_{ijk}$ is
antisymmetric. Thus, the sum vanishes.

\subsubsection{Invariance of the Hamiltonian}
The free particle Hamiltonian $\hat{H} = \frac{\hat{\mathbf{p}} \cdot
\hat{\mathbf{p}}}{2m}$ is a specific instance of a scalar operator (contraction
of vector $\hat{\mathbf{p}}$ with itself). Therefore:
\begin{equation}
  [\hat{L}_i, \hat{H}] = \frac{1}{2m} [\hat{L}_i, \hat{\mathbf{p}}^2] = 0.
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Time Independence}
Applying the Heisenberg equation of motion (or Ehrenfest's theorem):
\begin{equation}
  \frac{d}{dt} \hat{\mathbf{L}}_H(t) = \frac{1}{i\hbar} [\hat{\mathbf{L}},
  \hat{H}] + \frac{\partial \hat{\mathbf{L}}}{\partial t} = 0.
\end{equation}
This confirms that for an isotropic free particle, angular momentum is a strict
constant of motion, and the states may be simultaneously labeled by energy and
angular momentum quantum numbers.

\subsubsection{Classical Correspondence and Isomorphism}

The conservation law established above is the quantum manifestation of the
classical rotational invariance. Classically, the time evolution of angular
momentum is governed by the Poisson bracket with the Hamiltonian:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{d\mathbf{L}_{cl}}{dt} = \{ \mathbf{L}_{cl}, H_{cl} \}_{PB}.
\end{equation}
For a free particle, the absence of external torque implies $\{ \mathbf{L}_{cl},
H_{cl} \}_{PB} = 0$. The quantum result is structurally identical, following the
Dirac quantization correspondence:
\begin{equation}
  \lim_{\hbar \to 0} \frac{1}{i\hbar} [\hat{\mathbf{L}}, \hat{H}]
  \longrightarrow \{ \mathbf{L}_{cl}, H_{cl} \}_{PB}.
\end{equation}
This confirms that the role of $\hat{\mathbf{L}}$ is twofold and consistent
across regimes: it is simultaneously the physical observable corresponding to
classical angular momentum and the infinitesimal generator of the rotation group
$\mathrm{SO}(3)$ imposed by the isotropy of the vacuum.
