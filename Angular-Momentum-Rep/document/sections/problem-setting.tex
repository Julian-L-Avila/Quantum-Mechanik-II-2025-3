
\section{Problem Setting}

The purpose of this project is to construct a complete and self-contained formulation of the
angular-momentum representation for a nonrelativistic free particle. 
The central objective is to solve the time-dependent Schr\"odinger equation (TDSE)
\[
i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\ket{\psi(t)} 
= \hat{H} \ket{\psi(t)}, 
\qquad 
\hat{H} = \frac{\hat{\mathbf{p}}^{\,2}}{2m},
\]
in a basis that diagonalizes simultaneously the free Hamiltonian $\hat{H}$ and the angular-momentum
operators $\hat{L}^2$ and $\hat{L}_z$.

\vspace{0.3cm}
The motivation for this construction is threefold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item To reveal the role of rotational symmetry in quantum mechanics through the generators of the group SO(3).
    \item To establish a clear connection between spatial symmetries and conservation laws.
    \item To demonstrate how angular-momentum eigenstates provide a natural separation of variables for free-particle dynamics in spherical coordinates.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Physical Scenario}

We consider a single spinless particle of mass $m$ moving freely in three-dimensional space.
No external forces act on the system, so the Hamiltonian contains only kinetic energy:
\[
\hat{H} = \frac{\hat{\mathbf{p}}^{\,2}}{2m}.
\]

Because the dynamics is invariant under translations and rotations, the corresponding generators
$\hat{\mathbf{p}}$ and $\hat{\mathbf{L}}$ play a central role. In particular:
\[
[\hat{H}, \hat{\mathbf{L}}] = 0,
\]
which implies that $\hat{H}$, $\hat{L}^2$ and $\hat{L}_z$ admit simultaneous eigenstates.

\subsection{Goal of the Construction}

We aim to:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Construct the operators $\hat{\mathbf{L}} = \hat{\mathbf{r}} \times \hat{\mathbf{p}}$ from the canonical commutation relations.
    \item Express $\hat{\mathbf{L}}$ in both Cartesian and spherical coordinates.
    \item Establish the commutation relations that define the Lie algebra of SO(3):
    \[
    [\hat{L}_i, \hat{L}_j] = i\hbar\, \epsilon_{ijk}\hat{L}_k.
    \]
    \item Solve the eigenvalue problems for $\hat{L}_z$ and $\hat{L}^2$, obtaining the spherical harmonics $Y_{\ell m}(\theta, \phi)$.
    \item Expand the free-particle wavefunction in the basis $\ket{k,\ell,m}$.
    \item Solve the TDSE in this basis and reconstruct the wavefunction in position, momentum, and energy representations.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Mathematical Background Assumed}

The development assumes familiarity with:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Hilbert-space formalism: kets, operators, inner products.
    \item Canonical commutators $[\hat{x}_i, \hat{p}_j]=i\hbar\,\delta_{ij}$.
    \item Fourier transforms and the relation between position and momentum representations.
    \item Basic knowledge of spherical coordinates and special functions (Legendre polynomials).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Expected Outcome}

The final result of this construction is the full decomposition
\[
\ket{\psi(t)} 
= \sum_{\ell,m}\int_0^\infty \!\! dk\, k^2\,
\psi_{k\ell m}(0)\,
e^{-i\hbar k^2 t/(2m)}
\ket{k,\ell,m},
\]
along with the corresponding integral transforms
that yield the wavefunction in position-, momentum-, and energy-space representations.
This framework encapsulates the full rotational structure of the free particle and sets the stage
for generalizations to interacting systems, spin, and relativistic dynamics.
