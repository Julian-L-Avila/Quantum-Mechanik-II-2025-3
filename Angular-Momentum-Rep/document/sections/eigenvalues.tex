\section{Eigenvalue Problem of \texorpdfstring{$\hat{L}^2$}{L²} and
\texorpdfstring{$\hat{L}_z$}{L\_z}}

The commutation $[\hat{L}^2, \hat{L}_z] = 0$ implies the existence of a common
eigenbasis for the Hilbert space of functions on the unit sphere $L^2(S^2)$. We
seek the simultaneous eigenfunctions $Y(\theta, \phi)$ satisfying:
\begin{align}
  \hat{L}_z Y(\theta, \phi) &= \lambda Y(\theta, \phi), \\
  \hat{L}^2 Y(\theta, \phi) &= \mu Y(\theta, \phi).
\end{align}

\subsection{Spectrum of \texorpdfstring{$\hat{L}_z$}{L\_z}: Azimuthal Quantization}

The operator $\hat{L}_z = -i\hbar \partial_\phi$ depends solely on the azimuthal
angle. Separation of variables $Y(\theta, \phi) = f(\theta)\Phi(\phi)$ yields
the differential equation:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{d\Phi}{d\phi} = \frac{i\lambda}{\hbar} \Phi(\phi).
\end{equation}
The general solution is the complex exponential $\Phi(\phi) = A
e^{i(\lambda/\hbar)\phi}$.

\subsubsection{Topological Constraint}
The domain of $\phi$ is the circle $S^1$. For the wavefunction to be
single-valued (a necessary condition for the probability density to be
physically well-defined), it must satisfy periodic boundary conditions:
\begin{equation}
  \Phi(\phi + 2\pi) = \Phi(\phi) \implies e^{i(\lambda/\hbar)2\pi} = 1.
\end{equation}
This condition restricts the spectrum of $\hat{L}_z$ to discrete values:
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_m = m\hbar, \quad m \in \mathbb{Z}.
\end{equation}
Thus, the magnetic quantum number $m$ arises from the rotational symmetry of the
system and the topology of the configuration space.

\subsection{Spectrum of \texorpdfstring{$\hat{L}^2$}{L²}: Polar
Quantization}

Using the eigenvalue $\lambda_m$ and the spherical representation of
$\hat{L}^2$, the second eigenvalue equation becomes:
\begin{equation}
  -\hbar^2 \left[ \frac{1}{\sin\theta} \partial_\theta (\sin\theta
  \partial_\theta) - \frac{m^2}{\sin^2\theta} \right] f(\theta) = \mu f(\theta).
\end{equation}
Letting $\mu = \hbar^2 \nu$, this simplifies to the generalized Legendre
equation.

\subsubsection{Transformation to the Legendre Equation}
Introducing the variable $x = \cos\theta$ (mapping $\theta \in [0, \pi] \to x
\in [-1, 1]$), the equation transforms to:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:legendre_diff}
  \frac{d}{dx} \left[ (1-x^2) \frac{df}{dx} \right] + \left( \nu -
  \frac{m^2}{1-x^2} \right) f(x) = 0.
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Regularity Condition}
The differential equation (\ref{eq:legendre_diff}) has regular singular points
at the poles $x = \pm 1$. A Frobenius series analysis reveals that the general
solution diverges logarithmically at these boundaries unless the series
terminates. The condition for termination (and thus square-integrability) is
that the eigenvalue parameter $\nu$ must take the form:
\begin{equation}
  \nu = \ell(\ell+1), \quad \ell \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}.
\end{equation}
This quantizes the magnitude of angular momentum:
\begin{equation}
  \mu_\ell = \hbar^2 \ell(\ell+1).
\end{equation}

\subsection{The Spherical Harmonics \texorpdfstring{$Y_{\ell m}$}{Ylm}}

The non-singular solutions to the angular equation are the Associated Legendre
Polynomials $P_\ell^m(x)$.
\begin{equation}
  Y_{\ell m}(\theta, \phi) = (-1)^m \sqrt{\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}
  \frac{(\ell-m)!}{(\ell+m)!}} P_\ell^m(\cos\theta) e^{im\phi}.
\end{equation}
The phase factor $(-1)^m$ is the Condon-Shortley phase convention.

\subsubsection{Constraints on Quantum Numbers}
The structure of the solution space imposes the constraint $|m| \le \ell$. This
can be understood via the Rodrigues formula for the polynomials:
\begin{equation}
  P_\ell^m(x) \propto (1-x^2)^{m/2} \frac{d^{\ell+m}}{dx^{\ell+m}}
  (x^2-1)^\ell.
\end{equation}
Since $(x^2-1)^\ell$ is a polynomial of degree $2\ell$, derivatives of order
higher than $2\ell$ vanish. However, a more subtle constraint arises from the
definition of the angular momentum ladder operators $\hat{L}_\pm$. If $|m| >
\ell$, the state would have a norm less than zero, which is impossible in a
Hilbert space. Thus, for a fixed $\ell$:
\begin{equation}
  m \in \{-\ell, -\ell+1, \dots, \ell-1, \ell\}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Summary of the Angular Basis}

We have constructed a complete orthonormal basis $\ket{\ell, m}$ for the angular
variables. The defining actions of the operators are:
\begin{align}
  \hat{L}^2 \ket{\ell, m} &= \hbar^2 \ell(\ell+1) \ket{\ell, m}, \\
  \hat{L}_z \ket{\ell, m} &= \hbar m \ket{\ell, m}.
\end{align}
These states provide the necessary separation of variables to solve the radial
part of the Schrödinger equation for the free particle.
