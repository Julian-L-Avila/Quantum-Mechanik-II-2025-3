\section{Chiral Seesaw Mechanism}

As an alternative approach to explain the smallness of neutrino masses, we
revisit the mass generation mechanism proposed by Watson and Musielak within the
framework of the CDE \cite{watsonChiralSymmetryDirac2020}. In this formalism, the fermion
mass arises from Yukawa couplings to both a scalar field $\phi_1$
(Standard Model-like) and a pseudoscalar field $\phi_2$.
In the original proposal, restricted to pure left-handed fermionic fields, the
mass terms are shown to vanish or require fine-tuned cancellations. However, if
we assume the existence of a right-handed neutrino component $\nu_R$ (i.e. $\nu
= \nu_L + \nu_R$), the Dirac mass term does not vanish.
Instead, it acquires a complex phase determined by the chiral angle $\alpha$,
which is intrinsic to the CDE. To naturally suppress the neutrino mass without
relying on fine-tuning, we invoke the Majorana mass term, leading to a Chiral
Seesaw Mechanism.


\subsection{The Chiral Dirac Mass Term}

Following Watson and Musielak \cite{watsonChiralSymmetryDirac2020}, the general
Yukawa Lagrangian for a spinor field $\nu$ expanded about the vacuum expectation values (VEVs)
$v_1$ and $v_2$, for the fields $\phi_1, \phi_2$ respectively, is given by
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{Y} \approx -\frac{\lambda_{1}v_{1}}{\sqrt{2}}\overline{\nu}\nu
  - \frac{\lambda_{2}v_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}\overline{\nu}\gamma^{5}\nu.
\end{equation}

Defining the scalar mass parameter $M = \lambda_1 v_1 / \sqrt{2}$ and the
pseudoscalar mass parameter $\tilde{M} = \lambda_2 v_2 / \sqrt{2}$, and
decomposing the fields into their chiral components ($\nu_{L,R} =
P_{L,R}\nu$), the Dirac mass term becomes
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{Dirac}} = -\overline{\nu}_L \left(M + \tilde{M}\right)
  \nu_R - \overline{\nu}_R \left(M - \tilde{M}\right) \nu_L.
\end{equation}

In the CDE formalism, these parameters are related to the propagation mass $m$
and the chiral angle $\alpha$ by $M = m \cos \alpha$ and $\tilde{M} = -im \sin
\alpha$. Consequently, the effective Dirac mass coupling $M_D$ connecting
$\overline{\nu}_L$ and $\nu_R$ is identified as
\begin{equation}
  M_D \equiv M + \tilde{M} = m\left(\cos \alpha - i \sin \alpha\right) =
  m e^{-i\alpha}.
\end{equation}
This result is crucial, the Dirac mass in this model carries a physical phase
derived from the underlying chiral symmetry breaking.

\subsection{The Full Majorana-Dirac Lagrangian}

To implement the seesaw mechanism, we introduce Majorana mass terms. Unlike Dirac
terms which couple left- and right-handed fields ($\overline{\nu}_L
\nu_R$), Majorana mass terms violate lepton number by two units and must
couple fields of the same chirality but opposite charge conjugation properties.
Specifically, we utilize the charge-conjugated right-handed field $\nu_R^c =
C\overline{\nu}_R^T$, which behaves as a left-handed field under Lorentz
transformations. The most general Lorentz-invariant mass Lagrangian including
both the Chiral Dirac term derived above and the Majorana terms for left- and
right-handed neutrinos is
\begin{equation}
  -\mathcal{L}_{\text{Mass}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{Dirac}} + \mathcal{L}_{M_L}
  + \mathcal{L}_{M_R} +\text{h.c.}.
\end{equation}

Explicitly writing the couplings
\begin{equation}
  -\mathcal{L}_{\text{Mass}} = \overline{\nu}_L M_D \nu_R + \frac{1}{2}\overline{\nu_L^c} M_L \nu_L
  + \frac{1}{2}\overline{\nu_R^c} M_R \nu_R +\text{h.c.}.
\end{equation}

Here, $M_L$ represents the Majorana mass for the active left-handed neutrinos
---assumed to be negligible or zero in the minimal Type-I seesaw---, and
$M_R$ is the heavy Majorana mass scale for the sterile neutrinos.

\subsection{The Chiral Mass Matrix}

To find the physical mass eigenstates, we rewrite the Lagrangian using the basis
of left-handed fields. We define the interaction basis spinor $\Psi$ and its
charge conjugate $\Psi^c$ as
\begin{equation}
  \Psi = \begin{pmatrix} \nu_L \\ \nu_R^c \end{pmatrix}, \quad \text{and}
  \quad \Psi^c = \begin{pmatrix} \nu_L^c \\ \nu_R \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}

Consequently, the adjoint of the charge conjugate is the row vector
$\overline{\Psi^c} = \left(\overline{\nu_L^c}, \overline{\nu_R}\right)$.
Utilizing the Majorana identity $\overline{\nu}_L M_D \nu_R =
\frac{1}{2}\overline{\nu}_L M_D \nu_R + \frac{1}{2}\overline{\nu_R^c} M_D^T
\nu_L^c$, up to total derivatives, and the conjugated spinors
identity, the Lagrangian allows the explicit matrix expansion
\begin{equation}
  -\mathcal{L}_{\text{Mass}} = \frac{1}{2}
  \underbrace{
    \begin{pmatrix} \overline{\nu_L^c} & \overline{\nu_R} \end{pmatrix}
  }_{\overline{\Psi^c}}
  \begin{pmatrix}
    M_L & m e^{-2i\alpha} \\
    m e^{-2i\alpha} & M_R
  \end{pmatrix}
  \underbrace{
    \begin{pmatrix} \nu_L \\ \nu_R^c \end{pmatrix}
  }_{\Psi}
  +\text{h.c.}.
\end{equation}

This can be written in the concise matrix form
\begin{equation}
  -\mathcal{L}_{\text{Mass}} = \frac{1}{2} \overline{\Psi^c} \mathcal{M} \Psi
  + \text{h.c.},
\end{equation}
where the mass matrix $\mathcal{M}$ incorporates the chiral modifications from
the CDE
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{M} =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    M_L & M_D \\
    M_D^T & M_R
  \end{pmatrix}
  =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    M_L & m e^{-2i\alpha} \\
    m e^{-2i\alpha} & M_R
  \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{The Neutrino Chiral Mass}

To account for the smallness of neutrino masses, it is necessary to consider
that $M_R$ corresponds to the mass of a right-handed sterile neutrino. In this
framework, $M_R$ represents the interaction term, while the chiral mass mixes
the scalar and pseudoscalar components. We adopt the following identifications:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{$M_R$ (Right-handed Majorana Mass):} Generated by a
    fundamental coupling. This represents the heavy scale, where $M_R \gg m$.
  \item \textbf{$M_L$ (Left-handed Majorana Mass):} We assume
    $M_L \approx 0$.
  \item \textbf{$M_D$ (Chiral Dirac Mass):} The complete mass term
    from the CDE corresponds to the coupling $\overline{\nu}_L \nu_R$, which we
    identify explicitly as $m e^{-2i\alpha}$.
\end{enumerate}

Based on these assumptions, the mass matrix is given by
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{M} = \begin{pmatrix}
    0 & m e^{-2i\alpha} \\
    m e^{-2i\alpha} & M_R
  \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}

Finding the eigenvalues of the matrix in the usual way yields two mass terms.
By expanding the characteristic equation and applying the seesaw approximation
($m \ll M_R$), we obtain:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Heavy Mass ($m'_1$): Sterile Dominant State}
    \begin{equation}
      m'_1 \approx M_R + \frac{\left(m e^{-2i\alpha}\right)^2}{M_R} = M_R +
      \frac{m^2 e^{-4i\alpha}}{M_R}
    \end{equation}
    This Majorana state is dominated by the scale $M_R$ (sterile
    neutrino).

  \item \textbf{Light Mass ($m'_2$): Active Dominant State}
    \begin{equation}
      m'_2 \approx  \frac{\left(m e^{-2i\alpha}\right)^2}{M_R} =
      \frac{m^2 e^{-4i\alpha}}{M_R}
    \end{equation}
    This Majorana state corresponds to the active neutrino that is physically
    observed. The resulting mass term exhibits a complex phase factor
    $e^{-4i\alpha}$ dependent on the chiral angle $\alpha$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Mass Suppression and CP Violation}

The application of the Chiral Seesaw Mechanism yields the following expression
for the active neutrino mass
\begin{equation}
  m'_{\nu} \approx \frac{m^2}{M_R} e^{-4i\alpha}.
\end{equation}
This equation implies two significant physical results regarding the magnitude
and the phase of the neutrino mass.

\subsubsection{Mass Suppression}

The magnitude of the active neutrino mass is given by
\begin{equation}
  \left|m'_{\nu}\right| \approx \frac{m^2}{M_R}.
\end{equation}
This confirms the standard seesaw suppression mechanism, the observed mass is
inversely proportional to the scale of New Physics, $M_R$. For instance, if we
assume the electroweak scale $m \sim \qty{100}{\giga\electronvolt}$ and the
observed neutrino mass scale $\left|m'_{\nu}\right| \sim
\qty{0.1}{\electronvolt}$, the mechanism necessitates a heavy Majorana scale of
$M_R \sim \qty{e14}{\giga\electronvolt}$.

\subsubsection{The Chiral Phase and CP Violation}

A novel feature of this model is that the \textbf{phase} of the light neutrino
mass is directly determined by the chiral angle $\alpha$:
\begin{equation}
  \text{Phase}\left(m'_{\nu}\right) = e^{-4i\alpha}.
\end{equation}
This dependence has profound implications:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Leptonic CP Violation:} In a three-generation scenario, this
    complex phase translates into Majorana phases and potentially the Dirac
    phase within the PMNS mixing matrix. A value of $\alpha \neq k\pi/4$
    (where $k$ is an integer) implies a neutrino mass with a
    non-trivial complex phase, thereby introducing a novel and fundamental
    source of \textbf{leptonic CP violation}.
  \item \textbf{BSM Link:} This establishes a direct link between the
    theoretical angle $\alpha$ of the CDE and a physical observable
    (CP violation) mediated by the seesaw scale $M_R$. It suggests
    that the choice of the chiral basis becomes a physically observable property
    when lepton number violation occurs.
\end{itemize}

This constitutes a robust and novel result that successfully integrates the
theoretical framework of the CDE with the phenomenological success of the
Seesaw model.
