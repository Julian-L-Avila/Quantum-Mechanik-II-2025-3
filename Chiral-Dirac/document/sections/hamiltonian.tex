\section{Mathematical Background}
\label{sec:Mathematical Background}

We adopt the formalism of relativistic quantum field theory on a flat,
non-interacting Minkowski spacetime. The background geometry is defined by the
orthonormal basis $\left\{ \gamma^\mu \right\}$ satisfying the Clifford algebra
$\mathcal{C}\ell_{1,3}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)$, defined by the anticommutation
relation
\begin{equation}
	\left\{ \gamma^\mu, \gamma^\nu \right\} \equiv \gamma^\mu \gamma^\nu +
	\gamma^\nu \gamma^\mu = 2 \eta^{\mu \nu},
\end{equation}
where $\eta^{\mu \nu} = \text{diag}\left(1, -1, -1, -1\right)$ is the Minkowski
metric. Consequently, $\left(\gamma^0\right)^2 = 1$ and
$\left(\gamma^k\right)^2 = -1$. Greek indices $\mu = 0, \dots, 3$ denote
spacetime coordinates, while Latin indices $k = 1, \dots, 3$ denote spatial
components.

The chiral structure is determined by the pseudoscalar $\gamma^5 \equiv i
\gamma^0 \gamma^1 \gamma^2 \gamma^3$. Since the spacetime dimension is even,
$\gamma^5$ anticommutes with all vector generators $\gamma^\mu$ and squares to
the identity \cite{vazIntroductionCliffordAlgebras2016}. This permits the
construction of the orthogonal chiral projectors
\begin{equation}
	P_L = \frac{1}{2}\left(1 - \gamma^5\right), \quad P_R = \frac{1}{2}
	\left(1 + \gamma^5\right),
\end{equation}
satisfying $P_L + P_R = 1$ and $P_L P_R = 0$. A spinor field $\psi$ therefore
decomposes as $\psi = P_L \psi + P_R \psi \equiv \psi_L + \psi_R$.

\subsection{Hamiltonian Formulation of the Chiral Dirac Equation}

Following Watson and Musielak \cite{watsonChiralSymmetryDirac2020,
watsonChiralDiracEquation2021, Watson2022}, we consider the Chiral Dirac
Equation (CDE) for a spinor field $\psi$:
\begin{equation}
	\left(i \gamma^\mu \partial_\mu - m e^{- i \alpha \gamma^5}\right) \psi = 0.
	\label{eq:CDE}
\end{equation}
The corresponding effective Lagrangian density is given by
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal{L}_{\text{eff}} = \overline{\psi} \left(i \gamma^\mu \partial_\mu - m
	e^{- i \alpha \gamma^5}\right) \psi,
\end{equation}
where $\overline{\psi} \equiv \psi^\dagger \gamma^0$ is the Dirac adjoint. We derive
the Hamiltonian density $\mathcal{H}$ via the Legendre transform using the
canonical momentum $\pi = \partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{eff}} / \partial
\dot{\psi}$:
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal{H} = \pi \dot{\psi} - \mathcal{L}_{\text{eff}}.
\end{equation}
Explicit calculation yields
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal{H} = \psi^\dagger \left(- i \gamma^{0k} \partial_k + \gamma^0 m
	e^{-i \alpha \gamma^5}\right) \psi,
\end{equation}
where we have defined the Hermitian spatial generator $\gamma^{0k} \equiv
\gamma^0 \gamma^k$. The Hamiltonian operator $H$, defined by the evolution
equation $i \partial_0 \psi = H \psi$, is explicitly
\begin{equation}
	H = \gamma^{0k} p_k + \gamma^0 m e^{-i \alpha \gamma^5},
\end{equation}
where $p_k = -i \partial_k$ is the momentum operator.

To verify the physical consistency of the theory, we examine the Hermiticity of
$H$. We note that $\gamma^{0k}$ is Hermitian, and $\gamma^0$ and $\gamma^5$ are
Hermitian. However, $\gamma^0$ and $\gamma^5$ anticommute, $\left\{ \gamma^0,
\gamma^5 \right\} = 0$, which implies the commutation relation $e^{i \theta
\gamma^5} \gamma^0 = \gamma^0 e^{-i \theta \gamma^5}$. The Hermitian conjugate
is thus:
\begin{align}
	H^\dagger &= \left(\gamma^{0k} p_k\right)^\dagger + m \left(e^{-i \alpha
	\gamma^5}\right)^\dagger \left(\gamma^0\right)^\dagger \nonumber \\
						&= p_k \gamma^{0k} + m e^{i \alpha \gamma^5} \gamma^0 \nonumber \\
						&= \gamma^{0k} p_k + \gamma^0 m e^{-i \alpha \gamma^5} \nonumber \\
						&= H.
		\end{align}
		The Hamiltonian remains strictly Hermitian in the presence of the chiral phase
		$\alpha$, ensuring a real energy spectrum and unitary time evolution.
